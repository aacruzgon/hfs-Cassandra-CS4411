# ADR-anastasiabryz-0005: A search keeps up to 32 reads in flight, for its R1 reads and for its AND checks, fed from one stream across batches and slices

- **Date:** 2026-11-27
- **Status:** accepted
- **Context:**
    - ADR-anastasiabryz-0002 set the candidate cap at 100,000 distinct R1 reads per page or count, and left how long that takes unmeasured. Its value and setting are unchanged here; this entry adds how the reads are issued. `C/` is `crates/persistence/src/backends/cassandra/`.
    - **Alan measured it** on #133 (2026-11-27, https://github.com/aacruzgon/hfs-Cassandra-CS4411/pull/133#issuecomment-7229698732), on the CI machine with the repo's single node and three-node cluster at RF 3:
        - the timeout that binds is `HFS_REQUEST_TIMEOUT`, 30 s on the whole request; `HFS_CASSANDRA_REQUEST_TIMEOUT_MS` is per statement;
        - one node: every path answered 422 at 100,000 candidates inside 30 s;
        - three nodes, node-to-node round trips 0.53 to 2.04 ms: an index slice then its R1s 8 at a time took 17.2 to 53.3 s, over 30 s from 1.04 ms; slices streamed with 8 R1s in flight across them, 15.0 to 46.8 s; with 32 in flight, 5.6 to 14.3 s, at least 2× under 30 s at every level; one at a time, 29.4 to 338.7 s; `_id` 8 at a time, 41.3 s at the last level.
        - He recommended keeping 100,000 and keeping at least 32 R1 reads in flight across slice boundaries.
    - **The framework read 8 at a time, and not across boundaries everywhere.** The engine's driving plan (`plan_scan`, `C/search/engine.rs`) and `_id` (`live_by_id`, `C/search.rs`) already fed their R1 reads from one stream. The type scan of the unfiltered and `_lastUpdated` searches read 100 index rows, then all their R1s, then the next 100, so its reads drained at every batch and slice boundary.
    - **The AND checks ran one candidate at a time.** After R1, every candidate is checked against the search's other parameters: the compartment and date residuals on the loaded resource, and each token or URI parameter's `contains`, a clustering point read per partition and OR value (walkthrough 5.8). Those ran in the loop that consumes the R1 stream, one candidate at a time, so a search such as `code=X&status=Y` over about 100,000 candidates made about 100,000 `contains` reads in a row: Alan's one-at-a-time figures. An audit of the committed work (2026-11-27) found this.
- **Decision:**
    - A search has up to **32** reads in flight (`R1_CONCURRENCY` in `C/search.rs`; it was 8) at each of two stages:
        1. the R1 reads, charged to the cap in stream order;
        2. the checks against the other parameters: the driving plan's `residual`, the compartment, the date residuals and every other plan's `contains`.
    - Between the two, in stream order and one at a time, run the steps that are cheap and change shared state: the collapse of a repeated candidate, the tag check and the repair queue it fills, and the residuals on R1's own columns (the FHIR version, G34, and `_lastUpdated`). Both stages use `buffered`, so results stay in the stream's order and a page is cut in the same place.
    - Each read path feeds the first stage from one stream across its index batches and slices, so neither stage drains at a boundary: the type scan through `type_row_stream`, the engine's driving plan through its `stream`, and `_id` from its id list.
- **Alternatives considered:**
    - **Keep 8, and lower the cap.** Lost because, on Alan's figures, 8 at a time would need a cap of about 28,000 at a 2 ms round trip, below ADR 0002's floor of 70,003 (`large_id_set_suite`), and `_id` is slow at 8 whatever the cap.
    - **Stream across slices, but keep 8 in flight.** Lost because it measured 15.0 to 46.8 s, over 30 s at the last level.
    - **More than 32.** Not chosen: 32 kept at least 2× at every level measured, and every read in flight adds to the coordinator's load when many searches run at once, which isn't measured.
    - **R1 reads concurrent, the checks one at a time** (what the first version of this change did). Lost because every `contains` is a point read like R1, so an AND search would still make its reads one at a time.
    - **Run the tag check and repair queue concurrently too.** Not chosen: they make no read, and they change the repair queue and the previous candidate, which is simplest in one ordered step.
- **Consequences:**
    - A search of 100,000 candidates answers 422 inside 30 s on Alan's three-node figures. Separate machines, jitter, loss and concurrent load are not measured; Step 8 measures them, with how often the 422 fires (ADR-aacruzgon-0027's "To measure" list), and this number is revisited then. Alan is also considering refusing a search when the request's remaining time runs low, which would change G27 in his ADR-aacruzgon-0027.
    - When a page fills, reads beyond it have already started: up to 32 candidates in the check stage, and up to 32 more R1 reads behind them. Those R1 reads are point-read, so they count toward the cap, and the tag check has queued repairs for what the checked ones found; the page's answer doesn't change.
    - The extraction behind the compartment and date residuals is CPU work, not a read, so running it inside the second stage doesn't parallelise it.
    - `charged_r1` returns the id as a `String`: with a borrowed `&str` passed between the stages' closures, the search's `#[async_trait]` methods fail to compile (a higher-ranked lifetime error). That's one small allocation per `_id` value.
    - **A correction to ADR-anastasiabryz-0002**, which this log keeps append-only. Its Consequences say "Tests lower the cap through `CassandraConfig`". They don't: until Sam adds `HFS_CASSANDRA_SEARCH_MAX_CANDIDATES` to `C/config.rs`, the cap is the constant `DEFAULT_MAX_CANDIDATES`, and tests lower it through a test-only thread-local, `TEST_MAX_CANDIDATES`, which `max_candidates()` in `C/search.rs` reads and the tests' `TestCap` guard sets for one test. Once the setting exists, tests can set it through `CassandraConfig`.
